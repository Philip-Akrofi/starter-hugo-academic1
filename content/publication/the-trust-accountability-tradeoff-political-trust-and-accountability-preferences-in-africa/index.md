abstract: >-
  Political trust is commonly regarded as a desirable feature of democratic
  governance because it

  enhances legitimacy, political stability, and compliance with public institutions. Citizens expect

  trusted political actors to behave appropriately, even without monitoring, and may perceive

  democratic scrutiny of these actors as unnecessary. It is therefore plausible, as this paper argues,

  that trust in political actors may also be associated with lower support for measures that ensure

  accountable democratic governance. Using Afrobarometer survey data collected between 2019 and

  2023 across 37 African countries and ordered logit models, I find robust results suggesting that trust

  in the president and the ruling party is associated with lower support for accountable governance.

  Additionally, support for the ruling party is also associated with lower support for accountable

  governance, while preference for democracy is associated with higher support for accountable

  governance. The findings confirm the hypothesis that while political trust is important for

  democratic governance, it is nevertheless associated with lower support for measures that promote

  accountability.
